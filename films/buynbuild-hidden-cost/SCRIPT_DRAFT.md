## The idea in one line

A family gets a ₹25 lakh quote and ends up spending about ₹30 lakh, and **nobody cheated them**. The video hunts down where the missing ₹5 lakh went, leak by leak, with a running "LEAK METER" counter on screen that climbs from ₹25 L to ₹30 L.

## Why it should hold attention (viral mechanics)

1. **Hook in 5 seconds:** a big contradiction ("Nobody lied. The house still cost ₹5 lakh more.").
2. **Open loop:** "Leak number seven isn't made of cement. It's made of paper, and it can stop your bank loan." Promised at 0:30, paid off at 6:00.
3. **Running counter:** the LEAK METER on screen, so viewers stay to see the final number.
4. **Pattern break every 40–60 s:** a new chapter card, stamp, or "pause the video" moment.
5. **Pay-off + action:** a 6-question checklist viewers can screenshot, then the CTA.

> A family in Karnataka asks a contractor one simple question: how much will our house cost?

> So where did five lakh rupees go?

> And leak number seven isn't made of cement or steel. It's made of paper, and it can stop your bank loan cold.
> Stay till the end, because we'll give you the six questions that plug every one of them.

**ON SCREEN:** `house-cost-hero` (the half-built house at dusk). Counter slams **₹25,00,000**, then a red "LEAK METER" rolls toward **₹30,00,000** and freezes on **?**. Title card: **THE ₹5 LAKH NOBODY TOLD YOU ABOUT**.
*Wording note: "a family" is an illustrative example built from the blog's worked example, not a real case. A small on-screen label says "ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE".*

### 0:45 — How a quote really works · ~45 s

> Every house quote in India starts with one formula.
> Built-up area, times a rate per square foot.
> A thousand square feet, at two thousand five hundred rupees a square foot. Twenty-five lakh.
> Basic homes are often quoted around eighteen hundred to twenty-three hundred a square foot. Standard, up to about twenty-nine hundred. Premium, above that.
> That number feels like the price of your house.
> It isn't. It's the price of what's inside the contractor's rate. And a lot of your house lives outside it.

**ON SCREEN:** `cost-per-sqft-formula-diagram`, then code-drawn formula card **1,000 sq ft × ₹2,500 = ₹25 L**; three rate bands as cards (Basic / Standard / Premium, "indicative, Oct 2026").

### 1:30 — Leak 1: The area you're paying for · ~40 s

> Leak one is hiding in the word area.
> Contractors usually quote on built-up area: every floor, including the walls, the balcony and the staircase.
> But you live in carpet area, the floor you can actually walk on. And it's always smaller.
> So when two quotes say the same rate, ask: rate on which area? Get the answer in writing.
> It sounds small. On a full house, it changes the total by lakhs.

**ON SCREEN:** `carpet-area-hero`, then `carpet-area-formula-diagram`. Walls highlighted with a pen mark; stamp **"WHICH AREA?"**

### 2:10 — Leak 2: The list that isn't in the rate · ~60 s

> Leak two is the biggest, and it's a list.
> Plan approval fees. Soil test and structural design. Compound wall and gate. Borewell, sump and overhead tank. Electricity and water connections.
> Interiors, the modular kitchen, wardrobes.
> And GST, if it wasn't already in the quote.
> None of these are tricks. They're just not inside the per-square-foot rate.
> In our example, they add up to about three lakh.
> And then there's the money every builder needs and almost nobody budgets: a contingency, usually five to ten percent, for price rises and changes you'll make along the way.
> At eight percent, that's another two lakh twenty-four thousand.
> Twenty-five lakh just became thirty.

**ON SCREEN:** items tick onto a paper checklist one by one (with `building-plan-sanction-bbmp-bda-featured`, `soil-testing-featured`); **LEAK METER** climbs: **+₹3,00,000** then **+₹2,24,000** = **₹30,24,000**. Big stamp: **"₹25 L → ₹30 L"**.

### 3:10 — Leak 3: The ground under your feet · ~40 s

> Now the leaks that can push you past thirty.
> Leak three is the soil.
> Across north Karnataka, a lot of land sits on black cotton soil. It swells when it's wet and shrinks when it's dry.
> Build on it like normal ground, and you get cracks. Build on it properly, and the foundation costs more.
> That's why a soil test isn't paperwork. It's the cheapest insurance in the whole project.

**ON SCREEN:** `soil-testing-featured`, `footing-foundation-diagram`. Map card highlighting Kalaburagi / Raichur (blog district posts). Pen mark on the crack in the diagram.

### 3:50 — Leak 4: The bag and the bar · ~50 s

> Leak four happens quietly, at the material yard.
> Your quote says cement. But which cement? Thirty-three, forty-three or fifty-three grade? OPC or PPC? PPC is usually a little cheaper per bag, and both are fine when used right.
> Your quote says steel. But which TMT? Fe five hundred or five hundred D? Which brand?
> If the quote doesn't name the brand and grade, two contractors can quote the same price for two very different houses.
> So ask for an itemised quote: brand and grade of cement, steel, tiles, paint, wiring and plumbing.
> Now you're comparing the same house.

**ON SCREEN:** `cement-grades-33-43-53-diagram`, `ppc-cement-portland-pozzolana-cement-featured`, `tmt-bar-thermo-mechanically-treated-featured`, `steel-weight-formula-diagram`. Two quote cards side by side; highlighter on the blank "BRAND: ___".

### 4:40 — Leak 5: The concrete you pay for twice · ~45 s

> Leak five is the most painful, because you pay for it twice.
> When concrete isn't vibrated properly, it leaves gaps and pockets. Engineers call it honeycombing.
> And concrete that dries too fast never reaches its full strength. That's why it has to be cured, kept wet, for days.
> India's concrete code asks for at least seven days with ordinary cement, and ten with blended cements like PPC.
> Skip it, and the repair bill arrives later. With interest.

**ON SCREEN:** `honeycombing-in-concrete-featured`, `curing-of-concrete-featured`, `curing-of-concrete-diagram`. Counter **7 DAYS · OPC** / **10 DAYS · PPC**. Stamp **"PAID TWICE"**.

### 5:25 — Leak 6: The wall that turns white · ~30 s

> Leak six shows up a year after you move in.
> White, powdery patches on the walls. It's called efflorescence: salts carried up by moisture.
> A thin damp-proof course at plinth level helps stop that moisture. Leave it out, and you'll be repainting the same wall again and again.

**ON SCREEN:** `efflorescence-featured`, `damp-proof-course-dpc-diagram` with a pen arrow on the DPC layer.

### 5:55 — Leak 7: The paper (open loop paid off) · ~45 s

> And now, leak seven. The one made of paper.
> In Karnataka, the khata says your property is on the books. An A-khata is what banks are most comfortable lending against. B-khata properties can run into trouble with loans and approvals.
> In Bengaluru, an e-khata is now needed to register a property.
> Then comes the sanctioned plan, and at the end, the occupancy certificate, which many cities ask for before giving permanent connections.
> Get the paper wrong, and the cheapest house in the world can become the one you can't finance, can't sell, and can't finish.

**ON SCREEN:** `a-khata-vs-b-khata-diagram`, `e-khata-karnataka-featured`, `building-plan-sanction-bbmp-bda-featured`, `occupancy-certificate-oc-featured`. Big stamp **"LEAK #7: PAPER"**.

### 6:40 — The fix + CTA · ~30 s

> So here are the six questions that plug every leak.
> One: rate on built-up or carpet area?
> Two: what's not included in this rate?
> Three: has the soil been tested?
> Four: which brand and grade of every material?
> Five: who checks the concrete and the curing?
> Six: are the khata and the plan sanction clear?
> Ask these before you sign, and twenty-five lakh stays closer to twenty-five lakh.
> Prices, calculators and checklists for your district are on buynbuild dot blog.

**ON SCREEN:** six-item checklist card ("SCREENSHOT THIS"), each item ticking on its word. Final pull-back over the whole board. End card: **buynbuild.blog**.
