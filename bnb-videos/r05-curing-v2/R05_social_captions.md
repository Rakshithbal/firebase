Concrete curing explained: dry and hard does NOT mean your slab is done. 🏗️

Concrete doesn't get strong by drying. It gets strong by curing. Inside, cement reacts with water and builds strength slowly, day by day. If sun or wind takes that water away too early, the surface can crack and the slab can end up weaker than it was designed to be.

How to cure concrete:
💧 Ponding on the slab
🧱 Wet sacks on columns
🚿 Spraying on walls

How many days of curing? It depends on the cement and the weather. Ask your engineer for the number, write it down, and decide who waters it every single day.

Your slab looks dry and hard. That does not mean it is done.

Concrete gets strong by curing, not by drying. The cement inside keeps reacting with water, and that slow reaction is what builds strength. If sun or wind dries it out too early, the surface can crack and the concrete can end up weaker than it was designed to be.

Curing simply means keeping it wet and covered:
• Ponding water on the slab
• Wet sacks wrapped on columns
• Spraying water on walls

How many days? That depends on the cement and the weather, so ask your engineer for the number and write it down. Then fix who will water it every single day — you, the mason or the watchman.
