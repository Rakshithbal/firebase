What does M20 concrete mean? Your engineer says "use M20" — here's what that actually means. 🏗️

M stands for mix. The number is the strength grade the concrete is designed to reach, checked by testing small cubes of the concrete. A higher number means a stronger mix, and usually a higher cost. Your engineer picks the grade for each part of the house, based on the design.

But on site, the grade only holds if the mix is right: cement, sand, aggregate and water in the right amounts. Extra water makes concrete easier to pour, but weaker.

Your engineer says "use M20". What does that actually mean?

M stands for mix. The number is the strength grade of the concrete — the strength it's designed to reach, checked by testing small cubes. A higher number means a stronger mix, and usually a higher cost. Your engineer chooses the grade for each part of your house based on the design.

Here's the part most people miss: on site, the grade only holds if the mix is right. Cement, sand, aggregate and water, in the right amounts. Adding extra water makes it easier to pour — but weaker.

So before casting, ask your engineer two things:
1. Which grade goes where?
2. How will the mix be checked on site?
